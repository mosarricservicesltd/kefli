Fair use

Room for about 2,000 articles.

Kefli's server keeps up to 50 MB of your saves' text for free, and takes up to 20 MB a day. Past either, you can go on saving: each save is made and its link backed up as usual, and its text waits on your phone until it can be backed up too.

What's free

Every library has two allowances on Kefli's server:

  • 50 MB of backup space for your saves' text, about 2,000 typical articles. From there, each text is copied to a backup server that we don't run.
  • 20 MB of text a day, about 800 articles, counted over any 24 hours.

A typical article's text takes about 25 KB: a short news story a few kilobytes, a long essay 70 KB or more, and the whole of Alice's Adventures in Wonderland about 150 KB. At that size, two saves a day add up to about 18 MB in a year, and five a day to about 45 MB.

What counts

Your phone and your browsers share the same space and the same day. Two things count towards them:

  • Each save's text, encrypted on your phone before it's sent.
  • A page saved from your browser, which the extension sends to Kefli's server, encrypted, for your phone to collect. It counts towards the day as it's sent, so a save from your browser uses more of the day than one from your phone, and towards your space only until your phone has taken it. It's deleted from Kefli's server then, or after 14 days at the latest.

Bookmarks you bring in when you sign in with an account you already use are saves like any other, so a large import can use the day's 20 MB, and the rest of its text then goes up over the following days.

Nothing else counts. Kefli's server never stores pictures: your phone fetches them from the page when you first read it, and keeps them itself. A save's link, title, reading place and the rest of its record take a kilobyte or two, and are backed up whatever the space.

Past the limits

Once your backup space is full, or you've used the day's 20 MB, you can go on saving as usual:

  • the save is made, and its link, title and reading place are backed up like any other save's;
  • its text stays on your phone, where you can read it and listen to it as always;
  • Kefli tries again a day later, whenever the app is open, and until then Advanced counts the save as not backed up.

On your phone, the Saved sheet says so once Kefli knows the space is full or the day's 20 MB is used. A page you save from your browser still reaches your phone, which then fetches its text from the page itself, so a page that needs you to be signed in may come without its text.

A single text over 8 MB, more than a million words, is too long to back up at all. It stays on your phone, its link is backed up, and Advanced says so.

If you lose your phone

A text that hasn't been backed up is only on your phone. If you lost the phone, a new one would bring the save back with the rest of your library, and Kefli would fetch its text again from the page, which may have changed or gone by then.

Making room

Delete the saves you no longer need. Deleting a save deletes its text from Kefli's server, which frees its space straight away, and Kefli then sends the texts waiting for room that now fit.

Archiving a save doesn't free space, since an archived save keeps its text. The day's 20 MB counts what was sent, so deleting doesn't give any of it back.

Where to see it

In the app, go to You, then Advanced. Under Backup space, the row for Kefli's server shows how much of your 50 MB your library uses, such as 12.4 MB of 50 MB, and says when it's nearly full or full. When any save's text is waiting on your phone, Advanced also says how many saves, and why.
