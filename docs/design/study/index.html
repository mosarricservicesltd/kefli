How the study ran

You said the design is good but perhaps not cool or funky enough, and asked for a conceptual study of colours and themes: how to make a deliberately plain app bloody cool. Five directions each got their own board, drawn from the same rules. Every screen, flow, word of copy and control stayed exactly as the prototype has it; only the visual identity was free to change, from colour, type and texture to the icon and one signature move. Each board covers light and dark themes, three reading palettes with body text at 7:1 or better, the phone screens with the prototype’s real articles, the app icon, the extension’s save toast, and its own risks and costs. Three judges then scored all five, each through one lens: cool factor, daily use and access (with their own contrast and colour-blindness checks), and fit with Shelf and cost to build in Expo. I opened every board and screenshot myself before writing this page.

Today’s design, Soft pills, sits in the first column of the side-by-side below for comparison. The full prototypes it was drawn from are here too: the phone (every state is in its side panel) and the browser extension.

01

The recommendation

One direction as the base, with ideas taken from the other four. The decisions at the end are the parts only you can settle.

Build Paperback, and graft on the best ideas from the other four.

Paperback turns every save into a book. Its spine is coloured by how long the article is: mustard under 8 minutes, rose for 8 to 12, petrol for 13 and over. The unread part of the spine is a halftone screen, and whatever you have read or heard prints solid, so progress is something you can see from across a room, and Archive slowly fills with finished books.

It came first on cool and second on both daily use and fit and build. It is the only direction in the top two on every lens, and its total of 22 of 30 is the joint highest, level with Fingerprint since that board was re-scored (below). It also answers your question most directly, because the boring screens get the best treatment in the study: You opens on a little shelf of your own saves, the backup card is a bookplate, “Not written down” is a date stamp, and the first run is an empty shelf waiting for its first book. The Reader stays plain stock and Literata at 18 px, 12.4:1 or better in all three palettes. Terracotta survives at its exact value as Shelf’s one action colour, with white on it, so decision 61 holds.

What it costs. The gradient, the pills and the soft shadows go, so every component changes shape. The progress line turns on its side to become a spine on the card. The icon (decision 55) and two consistency-spec rules reopen. About a week to re-skin the prototype and the tokens; the spines need no Skia.

Level on points

Fingerprint was re-scored on 3 October after its screens were fixed, and it now ties Paperback on 22 of 30. It is first on daily use and second on cool, but third on fit and build, because it gives up Terracotta and the approved icon and puts a drawn tile on every row. Paperback stays my pick because it is the only board in the top two on every lens. With the totals level, the choice between the two is yours (decision 1).

Runner-up

If you would rather keep the pills, the icon and the shapes you have already approved, take Kiln, with Paperback’s halftone-to-solid progress on its line. It is the cheapest and safest of the five, and it answers “cool” less well: below its Clay masthead, the Saves list looks much like today’s. Fingerprint’s drawn covers are its strongest part and its chrome is close to stock, so its best ideas appear below as grafts. My pick is Paperback.

04

Grafts

The best ideas from the other boards, recast in Paperback’s flat ink and halftone so they belong to one system. None of them adds a screen, a control or a line of copy.

  • Fingerprint

    A drawn cover for saves with no picture

    Seed a small pattern from the save’s address hash and draw it in Paperback’s own inks: the article’s series ink, ink and paper, halftone for the unread part. It appears only where a picture is needed, on Now playing and the lock screen, so rows without a photo keep the full-width title the brief asks for. A restored library comes back with the same covers on any phone.

    Cost About 150 lines of shared TypeScript with golden fixtures in every platform’s CI. Two days.

  • Fingerprint

    Lock-screen artwork while listening

    Hand the article’s cover to Android’s media session as a 512 px image, so the lock screen and the notification show the book face-out while the voice reads.

    Cost Half a day.

  • Fingerprint

    Contrast guards in code

    Run the colour rules as shared fixtures: every series ink against every reading palette, the spoken-word tint kept at 7:1 for the text on it, every band at 3:1 against its surface, with a fallback to ink when a pair fails.

    Cost A day, then free on every change.

  • Instrument

    A word meter and a minutes scale on Now playing

    Print a ruler numbered in minutes along the cover’s band, and a row of small marks that print solid, one per spoken word, driven by expo-speech’s onBoundary. If the spike shows no positions, the meter stays blank and nothing is faked. Display only, with no knob, because v1 has no seeking (decision 48).

    Cost One to two days, after the spike.

  • InstrumentOverprint

    Buttons that press

    On press, a button drops 1 to 2 px against its keyline with a light haptic: Instrument’s key travel and Overprint’s press into register, in Paperback’s flat print.

    Cost An hour with expo-haptics.

  • Overprint

    Stamps for real states, used sparingly

    Paperback already stamps “Not written down”. The same treatment can carry “Couldn’t get the text” on a failed save. Only existing copy gets stamped: new words such as SAVED or PLAYED would have to go on the flow map first.

    Cost Trivial.

  • Overprint

    Flatten the decoration when the phone asks

    When high-contrast text or remove animations is on, the stamp straightens, the halftone becomes a flat tint and the binding wipe becomes a fade.

    Cost Half a day.

  • OverprintKiln

    Bake every picture once, in the WebView that already exists

    Make the two-colour cover in the hidden Readability WebView’s canvas when a save is fetched, cache a small JPEG, and let lists scroll plain images. That avoids adding react-native-skia, which costs several MB per ABI.

    Cost About a day, plus a check on the spike phone.

  • Kiln

    Counts as big numerals, and gentle changes of light

    Set “8 saves” and “12 listened” on You as large Jost numerals beside the little shelf. Crossfade any big change in brightness (theme or reading palette) over 150 ms, with opacity only under reduced motion.

    Cost Half a day.

Fix these whichever way you go

  • Errors always carry an icon and words

    Terracotta and error red are only ΔE 8.2 apart under deuteranopia, so colour alone can never say “error”.

  • A second cue for the spoken word

    Nearly every spoken-word tint in the study sits only 1.1 to 1.7:1 from its page. Add a 2 px underline in the line ink so colour-blind and low-vision listeners can follow the voice.

  • Snap the halftone to whole device pixels

    Check the 8 px screen for moiré on the spike phone while scrolling a 40-item list, in the dark theme too. At 6 px it read as a mesh on a 1x monitor.

  • Draw the screens no board drew

    None of the five shows onboarding (sign-in and the recovery flow), the extension popup or options page, or Speed read in full. Draw those in the chosen direction before the tokens freeze.

05

Your decisions

Five choices, each with its options and the answer I recommend. Once they are settled, the next step is the tokens, then the prototype re-skin and a spike check on covers and halftone.

  1. 1

    Which direction goes forward?

    Everything else on this page assumes the first answer.

    • RecommendedPaperback, with the grafts above
    • Kiln: keep today’s pills and icon, turn the colour up, and add Paperback’s halftone-to-solid progress to its line
    • Fingerprint as its board stands, level with Paperback on 22: ink-and-paper chrome, a drawn cover on every row, Terracotta and today’s icon retired
    • Fingerprint’s drawn covers on today’s Soft pills
    • Keep Soft pills as it is

    Why: Paperback is the only direction in the top two on every lens, and since Fingerprint’s re-score it shares the highest total. Its look comes from an idea that carries meaning (length and progress), which is harder to copy and slower to date than a style. Fingerprint matches it on points and wins on daily use, but it is third on fit and build because it gives up Terracotta and the icon; with the totals tied, this call is yours. Kiln is the honest fallback if reopening the shapes and the icon feels like too much.

  2. 2

    The app icon

    Decision 55 chose the Bookmark on Terracotta over the Ledge (books on a shelf). Paperback’s board proposes three spines.

    • RecommendedKeep the decision-55 bookmark, redrawn flat: the cream bookmark standing on a band that is solid on one side and halftone on the other, on flat Terracotta
    • Paperback’s board icon: three spines with the cream bookmark among them
    • Keep today’s icon unchanged, gradient and all

    Why: The bookmark is already proven at 16 px, and it already stands on a progress bar. Turning that bar into a spine band ties the icon to the new signature. The three spines read as a bar chart at 16 px and sit close to the Ledge you turned down, and a gradient icon would clash with a flat-ink app.

  3. 3

    Colour by length

    Spines are Mustard under 8 minutes, Rose for 8 to 12 and Petrol for 13 and over. The length is also written in the meta line.

    • RecommendedThree series inks by length, tested on a 40-item list before the tokens freeze
    • One ink for every spine, with only halftone and solid carrying progress
    • Colour by source

    Why: Length is something you can act on (a short one for the bus stop), and it is what makes the list readable from across a room. If the 40-item test tips into a children’s library, fall back to one ink. Colour by source means hundreds of colours that mean nothing. Petrol is a blue-green, so the brief’s “no blue or purple” line needs a note either way.

  4. 4

    Pictures in the list

    Paperback reprints article photos as two-colour covers in the series ink. The daily-use judge found that every recoloured thumbnail in the study costs some recognition.

    • RecommendedTwo-colour covers in the list and on Now playing, full colour in the Reader, then a week on your own library
    • Real colour photos everywhere, and let the spine carry the identity
    • Greyscale, with only the playing article in colour (Instrument’s rule)

    Why: Two-colour covers turn a mismatched pile of photos into one series, which is a large part of why the list looks designed. Recognition is a real cost, so test it on saves you know: if you stop finding articles by their picture, switch to real colour, which costs nothing to build.

  5. 5

    Shapes and capital labels

    Paperback retires pills for square corners and ink keylines, and sets sources, tabs, segments and chips in tracked Jost capitals. The consistency spec says radius 10 and no uppercase or letter-spacing.

    • RecommendedAdopt the square corners and keylines, keep capitals for one- or two-word labels (sources, tabs, segments), and amend the consistency spec
    • Adopt the shapes, keep every label in sentence case as the spec says today
    • Keep today’s pills under the new colours

    Why: The tracked capitals are the publisher’s voice that makes a spine read as a book, but Jost capitals at 13 px are hard work across a whole interface, so hold them to short labels. Pills under paperback colours would mix two systems and lose both.
