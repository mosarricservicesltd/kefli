Shelf · design studyDirection: Terracotta, turned up

Kiln

Shelf’s own Terracotta, fired hotter: whole screens poured in clay and char, a wide poster face for every headline, and a reading page left calm.

01

The concept

Soft pills already owns a colour most apps would never risk: a burnt clay orange with a cream bookmark. Kiln uses it as a material. Screens that announce something are poured in Clay: the Saves masthead, an empty shelf, a page landing from the browser. Everything to do with sound lives in Char, a deep fired brown, like a radio dial in a dark room. Lists and settings sit on cream paper as matte white tiles, and anything you can press is glazed. A wide, heavy grotesk does the shouting in titles and numbers; Literata does the reading. Shelf has no feed, no covers and no social layer, so colour and type are the only voice it has. Kiln lets that voice run at full volume everywhere except the page you read on, which stays as quiet as a library.

Paperback and LP sleeves, 1968–78Flat fields of one warm colour with a wide, heavy grotesk set huge. Gives Kiln its poured mastheads and poster titles.
Studio potteryMatte bisque beside glossy glaze. Gives the rule that content is unglazed and anything you can press is glazed.
Plaster walls at duskShadows that go brown and warm, never grey. Every shadow and hairline in Kiln is tinted with clay.
Hi-fi at nightOne warm glow in a dark room. The Listen tab becomes a Char room with Terracotta controls.
Match-day programme numeralsExpanded, heavy figures used as graphics. The counts on You and the clock on Now playing are set this way.

What stays, what changesThe low-risk route: every ingredient below is something Richard has already approved, at a lower volume.

Stays

  • Terracotta #B5542E as the fill, with white on it in every theme and reading palette (decision 61)
  • Cream paper #FCF7F6 and white cards in the light theme
  • Pills for every control, 48 px hit areas, labels vertically centred
  • The bookmark app icon and its geometry
  • Lucide icons, plus Shelf’s skip 15 pair and Speed read icon
  • One line per article for reading progress, read from one stored position
  • Every screen, flow, control and word of copy

Changes

  • Clay and Char become whole surfaces: mastheads, empty states, the Listen tab, toasts
  • Ink warms from #181D27 to #24130C, muted from #535862 to #6E5246, hairlines to #EEDFD7
  • Archivo replaces Inter and Roboto (100 % width for UI, 125 % for display); Literata replaces Noto Serif
  • The gradient becomes a glaze, with a highlight and a warm shadow, on every primary and play button
  • Card radius 10 → 14; sheets and mastheads 24 → 28; shadows tinted brown
  • Thumbnails duotone; the progress line becomes the pour
  • The tab bar is Char; the toast is Clay in place of the inverted pill
  • Dark theme warms from blue-black #0C0E12 to #140B07

The system

Four surfaces, one fill rule, two type families, and one moving idea. Hex values are the proposal; ratios are measured (WCAG 2.1 relative luminance).

Four firingsEvery screen region is one of four surfaces, and each surface carries its own text, muted, accent and line roles. Each tab gets a firing of its own, so you know where you are before you read a word.

You masthead

One rule for content on the fill. On Clay, glaze or the pour, content is white. Secondary text on Clay is white too, never a tint; hierarchy comes from size and weight. The signature move: the pourDrag the position. One stored position, four surfaces, one behaviour.

An article has one stored position, and Kiln draws it as Terracotta poured in from the left. On a card it is a 6 px shelf under the title, the same two-tone bar the bookmark stands on in the app icon, so every card stands on its shelf. In the Reader the whole top bar fills as you read, and every icon or letter the pour reaches turns white. While you listen, the pour moves into the player at the bottom and the top bar goes quiet, so one position still has one indicator; the mini player on every other screen pours the same way. Speed read and the lock screen keep a plain bar. It is built as two copies of the same row, the upper one clipped to the position, so it costs one width animation and nothing else.

The screens

Real size, 360 × 760 CSS px, drawn in HTML and CSS. The prototype’s copy, articles and controls, unchanged; only the identity moves.

SavesUnread · light

The masthead is poured Clay with the title at 125 % width; it folds into a 56 px Clay bar as the list scrolls. Cards are matte white tiles on cream. The part-read card stands on its shelf, the same two-tone bar as the icon. The mini player is Char, and its pour has passed the play button.

ReaderListening · Bisque

The page stays quiet: Literata 18/1.6 on #FBF6F2 at 15.6:1, the spoken word on a peach highlight. The dock carries the pour: everything left of 34 % has turned white, including half of the clock. The top bar goes plain while the dock plays.

ListenNow playing

Listening gets its own room, Char in both themes. Now playing is a Clay card with the elapsed time as a programme numeral and a white glazed play button. Played rows are Char tiles whose thumbnails glow ember.

Saves, first runEmpty

With nothing to list, the masthead pours down the whole screen. The icon’s bookmark stands behind it, enormous and tone on tone. The share-sheet drawing is a cream label stuck on at an angle. The one button is white, because content on Clay is white.

YouSettings · light

The boring screen gets a Sand masthead with the counts as programme numerals and the shelf drawn as spines, one per save, filled as far as you’ve read; archived saves are Char. The backup card is the screen’s one Clay moment. Rows keep their order and copy, icons in blush tiles. The spines are an addition, so they go on the flow map before anything is built.

Boring screens, turned up

Empty states, settings and lists are where most of the app’s hours go, so Kiln spends its loudest moves there. The populated Saves list is the quietest screen in the set.

Each tab has its own firing: Saves is Clay, Listen is Char, You is Sand. The Char tab bar runs under all three and holds them together. The Reader belongs to none of them; it takes the reading palette.
SavesUnread · dark

Night Clay is fired deeper, #7A3018, so the masthead glows without flaring (white on it 9.3:1). Cards are #1F120C with a warm hairline, and thumbnails turn ember. The mini player lifts to #2E1A12 to clear the ground.

ReaderReading · Char palette

Not listening, so the pour lives in the top bar: deep clay with an ember leading edge, turning the back arrow and half the address white. Body #E9DDD5 on #17100C at 14.1:1; the link is ember at 8.1:1. Archive floats, unglazed.

Dark, fired warm

Today’s dark theme borrows a cool blue-black from the UI kit. Kiln’s dark is brown-black, #140B07, so the Terracotta fill sits in the same family as the ground and never looks like a warning light.

The Char reading palette deepens the pour to #7A3018 and marks its edge in ember, so a 56 px bar of colour at the top of the page stays dim at night and its position still reads at 7.2:1.

What we lose when we stop reading long things

By Megan Garber · 11 min read

There is a particular kind of attention that only long reading asks for. It is slower than scrolling and less rewarding at first, and it builds something that skimming never does.

For most of the past decade, the tools we use to read have been built to keep us moving: one more headline, one more thread. The read-it-later apps were the exception, and their quiet decline tells us something about what we chose to value.

What they offered was modest. A list. A clean page. A promise that the thing you meant to read would still be there when you had the time to read it properly.

05

Honest notes

What’s cool

You can spot it across a room

One colour used as a surface, in every screenshot and every store tile. Shelf reads as Shelf in a thumbnail, before anyone sees the name.

Each tab has a firing

Clay, Char and Sand tell you where you are before you read a word, with the Char tab bar running under all three.

The pour makes the one idea visible

Shelf already had one position per article. Kiln turns it into a moving, legible thing on the card, the Reader, the mini and the dock, and ties it to the icon.

The boring screens get the loudest moves

An empty shelf floods with Clay, You gets programme numerals and a drawn shelf, the settings rows get tiles. The populated list stays the calmest screen, because it is the one you use most.

Low risk

Same colour, same icon, same controls, same copy. Everything is a dial Richard has already approved, turned further.

What could go wrong

Fatigue

A Clay masthead on every open could tire. It folds to 56 px on scroll, Clay never sits behind long text, and the Reader is never drenched. If it still tires, Saves keeps a Clay bar and only empty states flood.

The narrowest pass

White on #B5542E is 4.92:1. Nothing lighter may sit behind text on Clay, which rules out highlights where words go. The pour splits words across two colour pairs; both pass, but test with TalkBack users that the split never reads as a glitch.

Red next to Terracotta

Error crimson #C42A3C sits close in hue. Errors always carry the alert icon and words, never colour alone.

Duotone hides pictures

Some people find an article by its photo. Test whether thumbnails need real colour; the Reader keeps it either way.

Tab flash and old decisions

Listen is dark in the light theme, so switching tabs swaps cream for Char. Crossfade the room over 150 ms (opacity only with reduced motion). Kiln reopens decisions 46 and 57 (toast and mini looks) and the spec’s radius 10 and medallion.

What it takes

Tokens, about two days

tokens.json grows from two themes of ten roles to four surfaces × two themes, plus three reading palettes with a pour colour each. The generator emits React Native theme objects and CSS custom properties.

Fonts, about 1 MB

React Native can’t set variation axes, so the app ships static instances through expo-font: Archivo Regular, Medium, SemiBold, SemiExpanded Bold and Expanded ExtraBold; Literata Regular, Italic and SemiBold. The website uses the variable woff2s; the extension bundles them locally.

The pour, about a day

One shared component: the row twice, the top copy in an overflow-hidden View whose width Reanimated drives from the stored position. pointerEvents none, hidden from TalkBack. No Skia. CSS uses clip-path.

Glaze, duotone, graphics

React Native 0.76+ boxShadow (new architecture) gives inset highlights and brown shadows; expo-linear-gradient paints the fields. Duotone runs once per thumbnail in a react-native-skia ColorMatrix and is cached as a JPEG, so lists draw a plain Image. Supergraphic and spines are react-native-svg. App icon: edit two SVGs and rerun build-icons.cjs.

Measured contrastEvery pair below was computed with WCAG 2.1 relative luminance. AA asks 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text and icons; reading text in Kiln asks 7:1.
