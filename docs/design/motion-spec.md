# Motion spec

How Kefli moves, on the phone and in the browser extension, for the Fingerprint direction. Written on 2026-10-04 by merging three judged candidate specs (Material, Springs and Restraint) and adapting them to the direction Richard chose that day (flow decisions 65 to 72). Status: draft. It extends the consistency spec's motion rule (one enter, one exit and the cover draw-in) to every interaction on the flow map, and it needs a decision before anything is built from it (see Before build).

**Base and grafts.** Restraint and Material tied at 23 of 30. Restraint is the base: it led on the access and consistency lenses, and it keeps the house enter and exit curves that the consistency spec and `ui-brief.md` still name and that the cover draw-in runs on. From Material it takes the carried line, the stillness of a line between surfaces that share its geometry, the ±15 nudge, the direction-aware rolls and most of its input and build rules. From Springs it takes Progress catch-up, the velocity handoff from a finger, the toast replaced in place and the player's controls arriving outward from a play button that never moves. Every weakness the judges raised is answered in the last two tables.

**Sources.** `consistency-spec.md`, `flow-decisions.md` (1 to 72), `ui-brief.md`, `shared/cover/README.md` and `cover-v1.js` (`ShelfCover.css`, `ShelfCover.motion`), `mockups/prototype.html`, `mockups/extension.html` and `shared/tokens/tokens.json` 0.2.1.

## The grammar

Fade by default, and travel only to show where something went. Two curves: the house enter curve for anything arriving or settling, the house exit curve for anything leaving, and linear only for clocks. One spring, critically damped, used only when a finger lets go. One stagger, the cover's 34 ms. Two signature moves carry the identity: a cover draws itself once, when its save lands, and each article's line is carried from surface to surface and catches up with the reading you have just done. Everything else is a 150 ms crossfade or a change in one frame. Only transform and opacity animate; colour changes are stacked layers crossfading.

## Principles

1. **Nothing waits for motion.** Every tap commits at once: the data is written, audio starts or stops, navigation begins, focus moves and the keyboard opens. Animation describes a change that has already happened, and any input interrupts it: it retargets from its current value (and velocity, after a finger), and a reversal takes only the remaining fraction of the reverse duration. Leaving layers take no input; arriving layers take input from their first frame. Nothing commits in an animation's completion callback.
2. **Fade by default, and travel only to show where something went.** A pushed screen went one step deeper (24 px from the right), a sheet came from the bottom edge, a toast or player rose into the bottom stack (24 px), a row left the list or came back, the player changed shape in place. Anything else is a 150 ms crossfade or a change in one frame. Two curves only: the house enter curve for whatever arrives or settles, the house exit curve for whatever leaves, every exit the exact reverse of its entrance, and linear only for clocks. One spring, critically damped, only when a finger lets go.
3. **A drawn cover is still.** A cover draws itself once, when its save lands in front of you, and after that it moves only with the thing it sits on (a row, a sheet, a toast, the player). It never scales, slides, tilts, pulses, parallaxes or draws again, a cover replaced by another cover crossfades, and only the first-run scaffold loops. The covers are the only colour in the app, so their one arrival is the most motion Kefli shows.
4. **One line per article, and it moves sparingly.** Each article's progress line, in its own ink, is one value carried across every surface at its place, never redrawn from zero. It moves by itself only for Catch-up and Finish, and it glides only on a jump the person asked for (±15, a sentence swipe, a restore step). Live progress is set directly, so it never lags the voice or the scroll.
5. **The system's values change in one frame; values a person steps roll once.** Clocks, counts, 'N min left', You's big counts, restore's 'N of M', Speed read's words and the spoken-word highlight change in one frame, in tabular figures where they are numbers. Speed, default speed and wpm, which a person steps, roll 6 px in the direction of the change: up when faster, down when slower, the wrap from 2× to 0.8× included.
6. **Never move what the reader is looking at unless their hand is on it.** No animated scroll on opening or returning; follow-scroll turns the page in steps and waits while a finger is down or a fling is running; content inserted above the viewport keeps what is visible still; a notice waits for a drag to end; a text-size change keeps the current word in place. Persistent chrome (tab bar, top bars, player, toast) never replays on a screen or tab change and moves only when the bottom stack changes, on the enter curve.
7. **Reduced motion keeps every meaning and drops the travel.** With Android's Remove animations or prefers-reduced-motion, every translate, scale, rotation and glide becomes an opacity change at the same duration, or an instant set for lines, gaps and scrolls. Covers appear drawn, the word meter lights without a transition, loops rest at full opacity (the Saves sync line breathes in place, since it has no words beside it), countdowns keep running and direct manipulation still follows the finger. In the build, read the setting at launch and on change, branch explicitly, and pass `ReduceMotion.Never` to the fallback fades, because Reanimated's default would skip them too.

## Signature 1: Drawn once

**What.** A save's cover draws itself once, as the save lands in front of you: the square starts as its flat ground, then each shape scales in from 30 % about its own cell, in hash order, 34 ms apart, over about a second. After that it is the same still face on every surface.

**Why.** The covers are Fingerprint's identity and the only colour in the app (decisions 65 and 66). Drawing once marks the moment a save becomes part of the library: you shared it, it arrived from your browser, or it came back in a restore. Because it never replays, the face you watched arrive is the face you find later on every device, which is the promise the hash already makes. The drawing lives in the generator, so the phone, the website and the extension draw the same arrival.

**Spec.**

- **The drawing.** The cover starts as its flat ground, which never animates. Each leaf (its square, its shape and its seam strips) then scales from 30 % about its own cell's centre while fading in, on the enter curve over 520 ms, leaf n starting n × 34 ms after the first, in the generator's drawing order (depth first, quarters in reading order). A cover lasts 520 + (leaves − 1) × 34 ms: about 0.93 s for a typical depth-3 cover of 13 leaves, and up to about 2.7 s for the rare cover of 64 one-unit leaves. The numbers come from `ShelfCover.motion.once` and `ShelfCover.css` and are never restated in app code.
- **Where it plays, once each.** The share sheet's saved item; the extension's toast and its popup; a new row that joins Saves while Saves is on screen; each restored row that lands on screen during a restore; the first save landing in the first-run empty state; Welcome's three fanned covers on first launch (Before build, item 2).
- **Where it never plays.** On scroll, on a tab switch, on a remount or a later render, after Undo, on a theme or palette change, or on the Reader's band, the Now playing band, the player's cover buttons, Listen rows, the quilt, Search results, the options page's Couldn't send rows and the lock screen's artwork. A save that joins a list while that list isn't on screen is simply there, drawn, the next time you look.
- **Once means once.** A local `drawnAt` per save id (never synced) records the drawing, so a restart or a recycled row never replays it. A render or remount mid-drawing carries on from where it was, from a start time kept by save id outside the component (the prototype's `--draw-t`). The surface that receives a save (share sheet, toast) and the list it later joins count separately, and the list draws only if it was on screen when the save joined.
- **Against its container.** The drawing starts on the frame the save lands, with its container's own enter (sheet, toast, row in), so the first leaves grow while the container settles and the rest land after it. Nothing waits for it: the row, sheet or toast takes input from its first frame, and a row swiped mid-drawing carries the drawing with it.
- **Marks that belong to the save land with its last leaf.** The share sheet's success check and the extension's corner mark (sent, waiting offline, couldn't send) land as the last leaf does, and never later than 1 s after the drawing starts, on `motion.mark`. The words already say what happened, so the mark is decoration and may arrive late.
- **Waiting and failure.** A save whose text is still being fetched waits drawn, with its waiting dot, and never loops (decision 69). A failed fetch crossfades the cover to its own shapes in the chrome's greys; it doesn't draw again. A cover replaced by another cover (the mini moving to the next article, Now playing advancing) crossfades.
- **The one loop.** The first-run scaffold's slot draws on `ShelfCover.motion.loop` while Saves is empty. Nothing else loops a cover.
- **Reduced motion.** Covers appear drawn; marks fade in over 150 ms without scaling.
- **Build, phone.** Covers are Skia drawings from `ShelfCover.leaves` (cover README). While a cover draws, record it as one Skia Picture per frame on the UI thread (`createPicture` in a derived value): for each leaf compute `p` and `e` as in `motion.draw`, then `saveLayer` with alpha `e` (so a leaf fades as one piece, like the SVG group), scale by `0.3 + 0.7e` about the cell's centre, draw the square, module and strips, and restore. One frame clock (`useFrameCallback`) drives every drawing cover on screen; never one `withTiming` per leaf. When the last leaf lands, keep the final picture in the cache by hash and size and stop the clock, so a still cover costs nothing. Eight covers drawing at once during a restore is the load to test on the mid-range phone.
- **Build, web and extension.** `ShelfCover.svg(hash, { draw: true })` with `ShelfCover.css`, keeping the same element across the toast's state changes so the drawing never restarts.

## Signature 2: One line, carried

**What.** Each article's progress line, in that article's ink, is one value carried from surface to surface: on the row's hairline, under the Reader's bar, in the player, under Speed read's bar and on Now playing's ruler. It is always at its place when a surface appears, it stays perfectly still where two surfaces share its geometry, and it moves by itself only to catch up with reading you have just done.

**Why.** Reading progress is the other half of Fingerprint's signature (`ui-brief.md`: one line per article in that article's ink), and one stored position drives every bar (decision 36; the consistency spec's player rules). A line that is never reset shows that reading, listening and Speed read share one place. Catch-up is the one moment Kefli acknowledges reading, using the line already on the row and the row the eye is already on, and Finish shows where an archived article went. Moving this rarely keeps both noticeable.

**Spec.**

- **Anatomy.** Every line (the 3 px row line on the hairline, the 2 px reading line, the mini's and the Reader tier's 2 px lines, Now playing's 3 px bar, Speed read's 2 px bar, Welcome's front card) is a full-width fill inside an overflow-hidden track, moved with translateX = (p − 1) × track width, so its rounded end keeps its shape. The fraction p lives in one shared value per article, fed by the stored word index and written by the speech clock, the Reader's scroll and Speed read; every bar reads it on the UI thread, so lists never re-render for progress. Its colour is the article's ink for the surface under it (`ShelfCover.lineInk`), so one line may change colour between surfaces.
- **Carried.** A surface shows the line at its place from its first frame: the Reader opens with its reading line at the saved place, and Speed read, the player and Now playing do the same. Nothing draws a line from zero. Two articles never share a line: switching article (Archive and next, Play all moving on, another row's play button) sets the new article's line without a glide, and a finished article's line returns to the start in one frame (decision 35).
- **Still where the geometry matches.** The Reader and Speed read both put the line 2 px under a 56 bar in the same reading palette, so it doesn't move while the modes fade. The mini and the Reader tier both put it at left and right 28, bottom 5, so it stays put through the handover; because the ink is chosen per surface, two stacked lines (the app surface's ink and the reading page's ink) crossfade over the handover's 250 ms.
- **One position, one indicator.** Listen in the Reader: the top reading line fades out on quick out as the Reader tier rises with its own line already at that fraction; ✕ hands it back.
- **Live progress is set directly.** Per word while listening, per scroll frame while reading, per word in Speed read. In an article of a few hundred words or more, a word moves a 328 px line by about a pixel or less, so it reads as smooth with no easing and never lags the voice. If the Android spike shows the voice reports paragraphs only, the line glides linearly across each paragraph's estimated duration instead of stepping.
- **Jumps the person asks for glide.** ±15, a Speed read sentence swipe and a restore step glide on `motion.enter`; for those 250 ms, direct sets are ignored or retarget to the newest, so the next word doesn't cut the glide short.
- **Catch-up.** When you come back to the list that opened the article (Saves, Search results, Listen rows), that row's line moves from where it stood when you opened the article to where it stands now, as the Reader clears it. Trigger: a pop to the opener (←, Android Back, a predictive-back commit) after the stored position moved by 2 % or more, by reading, listening or Speed read. Timing: `motion.catchUp`, starting 150 ms after the pop begins, 400 ms on the enter curve, done about 550 ms after the tap. 'N min left' changes once, in one frame, as it starts. It can run backwards if you scrolled back. A row read for the first time grows its line from the hairline's left end, since it stood at nothing. Skipped, with the line set at once, for a change under 2 %, a row off screen, a tab switch, a restore, Undo, a cancelled predictive back and a cold start. Once per return, on that row only. Live progress (audio still playing) or the reader's own scroll takes the line over from wherever it is.
- **Finish.** Archive from the Reader (floating Archive, end card, Speed read's done card): as the pop runs, the row's line completes to full, then the row leaves by row out. From the tap: pop 0–200 ms, fill 100–300 ms, row fades 300–400 ms, gap closes 400–600 ms. The Archived toast rose at the tap. The leaving row takes no taps; the rows below take taps throughout. Undo before the end cancels it and the row keeps its old line. Back to Unread and Delete skip the fill. A row off screen is removed without motion.
- **Reduced motion.** The line keeps carrying its information: surfaces show it at its place, Catch-up, Finish and jumps set it at once, live progress is unchanged, and the handover's stacked lines still crossfade, since that is opacity. Finish becomes a 200 ms fade of the row with the gap closing at once.
- **Build.** On opening an article, store `{ id, pBefore }`. On the opener's focus (`useFocusEffect`), check the row is visible (`onViewableItemsChanged`), then run `withDelay(150, withTiming(pNow, { duration: 400, easing }))` on that row's shared value, gated on the JS stack's `transitionStart` event or a 150 ms timer. Prototype: `.progress i`, `.rprog i`, `.rsvp .rbar i`, `.ruler .pline i`, `.hc .pbar i` and both countdowns move from width to transform.

## Don'ts

1. Don't animate width, height, top, bottom, left, margin, padding, border width, font size, corner radius or blur in the build, and don't animate colour. Colour changes are stacked layers crossfading: selection fills, error edges, reading palettes, a line's ink, the player's surface. The prototype's transitions on `bottom`, `height`, `width`, `left`, `top`, `padding-bottom` and `background` become transforms, plates and FLIP.
2. Don't draw progress or countdowns with width or scaleX. Translate a full-width fill inside a clipped track, so a rounded end keeps its shape.
3. Don't draw a cover more than once, on scroll, on a tab switch, on a remount, after Undo or on a palette change, and never draw the band, the quilt, the player's cover buttons, Listen rows or the lock screen's artwork. Never scale, slide, tilt, pulse, parallax or tint a drawn cover, and never loop one outside the first-run scaffold.
4. Don't fade a progress line out and redraw it from zero, don't draw the Reader's line on open, and don't carry a line between two different articles.
5. No overshoot, bounce, wobble or shake. The one spring is critically damped and clamped, and errors say what's wrong at the field.
6. No screen-level scale, zoom, parallax or container transform. The screen beneath a push stays still.
7. No stagger other than the cover's 34 ms, which the Reader tier's three controls reuse. Exits never stagger.
8. Don't animate values the system changes: clocks, counts, 'N min left', You's big counts, 'N of M', Speed read words and the spoken-word highlight change in one frame. Only values a person steps roll.
9. No looping motion outside waiting and the first-run scaffold: no pulsing play button, breathing player, equaliser on a Listen row or animated gradient. Waiting loops start after 150 ms.
10. No celebration: no confetti, burst, streak or sound on All read, the end of an article, a finished restore or Speed read's done card. An earned mark is the most Kefli does.
11. No urgency on countdowns: a QR or pairing code never pulses, flashes or turns red, and a live QR never moves or scales.
12. Stamps never slam, scale, rotate or wobble. They arrive and leave with their line by crossfade, already turned −2°, and straight under prefers-contrast: more.
13. Don't replay persistent chrome on a screen or tab change, and never raise a toast over an open sheet.
14. No bare spinners (RefreshControl's included), no ripples or press-scale, no animated splash, app icon or toolbar icon, and no frame-swapped `setIcon`.
15. Don't commit state in an animation's completion callback, don't block taps on arriving layers, and don't queue animations: a second tap retargets the one in flight.
16. Don't animate focus rings or move focus late. Focus lands on the frame of the change.
17. Don't scroll for the reader when they didn't ask, and never while a finger is down or a fling is running.
18. Don't use React Native's LayoutAnimation, or JS-thread Animated for anything a finger drives. The JS stack's screen transitions are the one Animated use, and their interpolators branch on the reduced-motion flag themselves.
19. Don't add motion that implies behaviour that isn't on the flow map: swiping between segments, swiping away a toast or the mini, pull to refresh, seeking on a bar (decision 48), long-press previews.
20. Don't build from this spec before its paperwork is done (Before build, item 1).

## Before build

1. **Adopt the spec.** Record it as a flow decision; amend the consistency spec's 'Motion uses one enter and one exit' bullet to point here; add the new tokens (quick, reflow, mark, release, catch-up, countdown, ambient) to the motion group in `shared/tokens/tokens.json` 0.2.2 with a changelog entry, generated to CSS custom properties and a TypeScript module for Reanimated, in the same change. CLAUDE.md's process rule asks for this before anything is built.
2. **Welcome's covers drawing on first launch.** Decision 69 and the consistency spec's Covers section list where covers draw, and Welcome isn't among them.
3. **Follow-scroll's resume rule.** Following waits while the reader is away from the spoken word and resumes once it is on screen again. It refines decision 18 and goes on the flow map.
4. **Marks land with the last leaf, at most 1 s in.** This changes the extension mockup's `paintLead`, which waits for the last leaf however long the cover takes.
5. **The Reader tier's controls shift left when ✕ appears on pause** (decisions 20 and 45). Motion softens it; reserving the ✕ slot would remove it. Richard's call.
6. **Prototype changes once accepted.** Lines and countdowns from width to transform; `.w` loses its transition; `.card`'s swipe return takes the release spring's `linear()`; `.cardwrap` collapse and expand become row out and row in; `.refresh` moves by translateX, the scan line by translateY, `.eq` by scaleY; the toast's, mini's and dock's `bottom` and `.onbactions`' `padding-bottom` become transforms; the Aa palette changes through plates; the recovery blur becomes an opacity crossfade; tab content crossfades.
7. **Check in the Android spike.** `onBoundary` positions (the live line, the highlight, the word meter); predictive back in SDK 57's navigator; Skia drawing eight covers at once on the test phone; expo-image's transition on memory-cache hits; Reanimated's reduced-motion reading on Remove animations.
