Open choices

Four small decisions: three icons and one menu. Each option is drawn as it would appear in the app, in light and dark. Decided by Richard on 27 September 2026: C, A, A, B (flow decisions 62 and 63); the chosen option is outlined in Terracotta.

1. Speed read, in the Reader’s ⋯ menu

The menu row that opens speed reading. The label always says “Speed read”, so the icon only helps people spot the row.

Chosen: C. It draws the Speed read screen itself: the two rules and the focus ticks around the word, so the row previews what it opens. The lightning bolt says “fast” but could be anything quick, and the scan frame reads as “scan a document”.

2. The speed button in the player

The pill that cycles 0.8×, 1×, 1.2×, 1.5×, 2×. It works as text alone today. Shown in the Reader’s player while paused, its tightest state, where the ✕ also shows, twelve minutes into a long read.

Chosen: A (no icon). The number is the information, and every slot in the Reader’s player is already taken. An icon leaves 29 px for the time: enough up to 9:59, but from ten minutes on the time is cut off. Without one it fits up to 59:59.

3. A “shelf” icon

This icon was drawn for “your shelf” moments, but no screen currently needs it: the empty Saves screen has its own illustration and “All read” has its tick. The only candidate is the card that brings your saves back on a new phone.

Chosen: A (leave it out). The card’s title already says what’s happening, and an icon added only to fill space is weight. If you take all three recommendations, the app has two custom icons in total, speed read and the ±15 pair, and everything else is Lucide.

4. The row menu in Archive

In Archive, each article has a ⋯ button that offers Back to Unread and Delete. The two mockups show it differently, so one needs to change to match the other. Panel B is the real phone prototype.

A · Small menu beside the rowSaves card mockup

This was a small menu anchored under the ⋯, overlapping the row below. It was replaced by B on 27 September; the earlier drawing is in the Saves card mockup’s history (commit ca59025).

  • Appears next to your finger, with no dimmed background.
  • Android’s usual pattern for a ⋯ on a list item.
  • Behaves differently from the Reader’s ⋯, which opens a sheet.
  • Smaller targets, and it can cover the rows beneath it.
B · Sheet from the bottomPhone prototypeChosen
  • Matches the Reader’s ⋯ menu and every other menu in the app.
  • Headed with the article’s title, so you can see which one you’re acting on.
  • Full-width rows, within easy reach of your thumb.
  • One extra motion: the sheet slides up over a dimmed screen.

Chosen: B. One kind of menu throughout the app is easier to learn, and the title heading guards against deleting the wrong article. The Saves card mockup now matches.
