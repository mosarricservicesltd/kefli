bitcoinmagazine.com
Why relays, not servers, are the future of personal data
Rowan Pike · 6 min · Saved yesterday

The first time a page disappears from the web while it is still in your list, you learn what a read-it-later app is actually for. Nate Weiner learned it in 2007, when a bookmark folder full of dead links pushed him to build a Firefox extension that kept the text as well as the address.

Everything that followed was an elaboration of that one move. Sync arrived so the reading could happen somewhere other than the saving. A clean view arrived so the text could be read without the page fighting back. A voice arrived so the reading could happen with your hands on a steering wheel.

What never changed was the shape of the shelf: a list, newest at the top, that belonged to one person and nobody else.
