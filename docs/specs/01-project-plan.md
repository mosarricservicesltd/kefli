## 1. What we are building

A free read-it-later app, phone-first, whose library lives on nostr under the user's own key so that it outlives the service. Version 1 is: an Android app, an iOS app, a browser extension for Chrome and Firefox, and a small operator backend (relay, blob store, HTTP API). Saves are private by default. Listening works from the first minute using the phone's own voice; server-generated voices come later (flow decision 22). There is no discovery, no social layer, no tags and no signup in v1. The full decision record is in the research brief, the flow map and the adversarial review; this document assumes them.

## 2. Team and ownership

Five roles, one person each in the plan, understood to be Richard plus Claude Code wearing different hats in practice, which is why the documents are written as independent specs with a hard contract between them: the discipline of separate ownership is what keeps changes from leaking across boundaries.

The project engineer owns the contract (`shared/contract/contract.md`), the design system tokens, the shared fixtures, the sequence, and sign-off at each gate. The backend engineer owns the VPS, relay, Blossom and the HTTP API. The Android engineer owns the Android app and its share target. The iOS engineer owns the iOS app and its share extension. The extension engineer owns the Chrome and Firefox extension and the pairing protocol with the phone.

## 3. Sequence

The order is chosen so that each stage produces something Richard can use, and so that the piece with the longest external dependency (Apple review) starts as early as it can without blocking anything.

**Stage 0 — Design and contract (week 0).** UX flow is done. This stage produces the UI design: the token file (colour direction chosen from the four, type scale, spacing, radii, one accent), and the screen set drawn phone-sized: Library (empty, populated, offline), Reader with mini player, Saved sheet, backup card and recovery sheet, You tab, sign-in for existing users, extension pairing screen, extension toast. Design is reviewed by Richard against the flow map and frozen. The shared fixtures are written. The contract is signed off. Exit gate: Richard approves the screens; every engineer has read the contract and raised objections; fixtures exist.

**Stage 1 — Backend foundation and Android core (weeks 1–2).** Backend brings up the VPS with strfry (write allowlist, NIP-42 for gift-wrap reads), blossom-server with quotas, the API skeleton with `/register` and `/account`, TLS, backups and monitoring. Android builds key generation, the signer interface with the local provider, the share target, on-device parsing to Markdown, encryption and publish of kind 30078, Blossom upload, the Library, the Reader, swipe-to-archive with undo, offline cache, and SystemVoice playback with word highlighting. Exit gate: Richard saves from X and Chrome on his Android phone, reads offline, listens with the system voice, archives, and the events verify on the relay with the fixture decryption tool.

**Stage 2 — Extension and Android completion (weeks 3–4).** Extension engineer ships the Chrome build with pairing by QR, Readability capture, gift-wrapped save requests, and the Firefox build. Android adds the gift-wrap listener, the mini player with lock-screen controls, the Listen tab, the backup flow, second-device pairing, NIP-55 and NIP-46 sign-in, existing-bookmark import, and "Delete my data". Exit gate: end-to-end save from a laptop appears on the phone within ten seconds, measured with the app open (flow decision 84); an article plays for an hour with the screen locked on battery, its lock-screen controls working throughout, and after unlocking the highlight is on the word being spoken; a second Android device pairs and shows the library; Play Store internal test track live. The locked hour replaces the server-audio gate ("server audio plays with highlighting"), since v1 listens with the phone's own voice only (flow decisions 22 and 87).

**Stage 3 — iOS (weeks 4–6, overlapping stage 2).** iOS engineer starts at the beginning of week 4 with the Android app as reference and the contract as law: keychain with iCloud sync, share extension with the Safari preprocessing script and the URL-only fallback, Library, Reader, SystemVoice, mini player with Now Playing, Listen tab, backup, pairing, NIP-46 sign-in, delete. Exit gate: same as stage 1 and 2 gates on an iPhone; TestFlight build distributed.

**Stage 4 — Integration and closed beta (week 6–7).** The browser extension is distributed to beta testers in developer mode (unpacked on Chromium browsers, a signed unlisted build on Firefox), per extension spec section 11; store listings are filed now and published at Stage 5. Ten to twenty friendly users across both platforms. The project engineer runs the integration test matrix (section 6) and triages. No new features. Exit gate: no open severity-1 issues; the three offline scenarios pass on both platforms; store listings, privacy labels and account-deletion flow ready.

**Stage 5 — Store submission and launch (week 7–8+).** Play Store production, App Store submission with a pre-written note to the reviewer covering account deletion and the reader. Calendar tail is Apple's. Launch is quiet: a post from Richard, the extension listings, nothing else.

## 4. Integration points, in the order they are exercised

The relay is the first shared surface: Android publishes kind 30078 saves in week 1 against the backend's allowlist and to the default public relays, which is where relay policy surprises surface. Blossom upload is second. The gift-wrap channel between extension and phone is third, in week 3, and is the first time two engineers' code meets without the backend in the middle beyond storage. iOS integrates against all three at once with everything already proven by Android, which is why it goes last. The shared fixtures are how integration is checked without meetings: a platform that produces the fixture `text_hash` for all twenty pages will share the deferred audio cache with the others once server voices arrive (contract section 4.2), and one that does not would silently miss it, so the fixture suite runs in every platform's CI.

## 5. Design system, decided before code

Colour direction: to be chosen by Richard from Paper, Ink, Sage, Ocean. Whichever is chosen, the token file has exactly: background, surface, line, text, muted, accent, accent-soft, plus semantic success and error; a type scale of 13, 15, 17, 20, 28, 32; spacing unit 4 with a scale of 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32; one radius of 10 for containers and 999 for pills; system font on both platforms. Reader typography is separate from UI typography: body 18 with line height 1.55, serif on iOS (New York) and Android (Noto Serif), with the Aa sheet offering size and light/sepia/dark only. Adding a token requires a written reason in the token file's changelog. Icons are a single stroke set (Lucide) on all platforms.

## 6. Testing at each stage

Unit tests on every platform for: normalise (fixtures), Markdown conversion (fixtures), NIP-44 self-encryption round trip (fixtures), NIP-98 signing, and the item JSON schema. Contract tests run by the project engineer against a staging relay and server before each gate: publish, auth-required read, unauthorised read refused, Blossom upload with quota, gift wrap round trip. Device tests, manual, recorded as a checklist per platform: share from five apps (browser, X, Mail, a newsletter app, a PDF viewer), save while offline then reconnect, kill the app mid-save, lock screen controls, Bluetooth car controls, battery saver on, reinstall and confirm the key returns via credential sync, restore on a second device from the recovery code, delete everything. Parser tests use the twenty fixtures plus a live list of thirty URLs (news, Substack, Medium, GitHub README, Wikipedia, paywalled NYT, X thread, YouTube page, PDF link, a JavaScript-only SPA) with expected outcomes including the honest failures. Beta testing in stage 4 uses a one-page script and a form; issues triaged into severity 1 (data loss, crash, privacy), 2 (flow broken), 3 (polish).

## 7. Risks the project engineer is watching

The two fatal findings from the review are closed by design: no plaintext article or audio is ever publicly addressable, and the timeline assumes AI-assisted build speed with human-speed testing. The remaining risks are: iOS share extension memory on JavaScript-heavy pages (mitigated by the Safari preprocessing script and the honest "text pending" state); word-highlight drift on long articles (mitigated by re-anchoring highlights at paragraph boundaries, contract section 7); NIP-46 reliability with third-party bunkers (mitigated by treating it as an existing-user path only, with local key as the default); Apple review under 5.1.1(v) (mitigated by shipping deletion in the first submission and writing the reviewer note); and scope creep, which the contract and the flow map exist to refuse. Anything not in the flow map is v2 and goes on a list, not into a sprint.

## 8. Calendar (AI-assisted build, human-speed testing, evenings and weekends)

Week 0 design and contract. Weeks 1–2 backend foundation and Android core. Weeks 3–4 extension and Android completion; iOS starts week 4. Weeks 4–6 iOS. Weeks 6–7 beta. Weeks 7–8 submissions. Apple's review adds one to three weeks of calendar at the end that we do not control. If Richard takes a fortnight off, stages 1 and 2 compress to about ten days and everything shifts left by two weeks.

## 9. What "done" means for v1

Richard and a dozen friends use it daily for a month on both platforms; a save from any of the five test apps reads offline on the train; listening works with no account and no download; the backup and second-device flows have been exercised by someone other than Richard; the library survives a reinstall; and the operator server can be turned off for an hour without the app losing any function except making new saves (contract section 1).
